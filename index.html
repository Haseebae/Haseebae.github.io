Approach

Using agents is easy. Evaluating them, and the systems they run on, is hard, and what "good" means changes from one application to the next.

  1. 01

    A research mindset

    I formalise the problem before building, benchmark against public baselines, and read agent traces rather than stopping at a leaderboard score. Claims come with reproducible experiments.

  2. 02

    End-to-end ownership

    Problem statement, agent, tool environment, infrastructure, evaluation, and then the paper or the production deploy. Tech lead on four production agents at Harman; first author on FAME.

  3. 03

    Systems foundations

    Serverless orchestration on AWS, FSDP and DeepSpeed training, Spark over 4 TB of Common Crawl, federated learning on real edge hardware. Agents are distributed systems, and I treat them that way.

Benchmarks & research workloads

  • MLE-Bench · coding & ML-training agents

    Autonomous ML engineering that runs for hours

    Agents (MLZero) that write, run and debug Kaggle-style training code inside a coding sandbox I built and managed. Tool calls that block for minutes to hours break synchronous serverless orchestration, so FAME checkpoints agent and orchestrator state and resumes on callback without billing idle waits.

    Completes every iteration where Step Functions time out · 3.64–4.24× cheaper than AWS Durable Functions (agent level), 2.67–8.23× (orchestrator level)

  • Microgrid power forecasting · research agents

    Agents that tune federated learning on real devices

    LLM-guided evolutionary search (OpenEvolve, AdaEvolve, Auto-FL-Research) tuning ML and systems knobs jointly, on Raspberry Pis, Jetsons and in simulation. The hard part: the sim-to-real gap, and cost-to-good-enough vs. cost-to-best.

    Also AIITS traffic analytics and CIFAR-100 · first author, in prep. with IBM Research

  • LiveMCPBench · tool use at scale

    Agents picking from hundreds of live MCP tools

    Long tool outputs bloat context and cost. I cut MCP overhead with S3 handle passing and tool-output caching, and mapped ReAct, Reflexion and LLM Compiler onto stateless functions.

    Used in FAME (TSC, under review) and ICWS 2026

  • τ-bench · conversational agents

    Multi-turn agents under policy, with simulated users

    Retail and airline customer-service tasks where the agent must hold state across a conversation and follow domain rules. A sharp test of running stateful agents on stateless infrastructure.

    Used in FAME, ICWS 2026 and the durability work

  • GAIA · general assistants

    Durability when the plan is decided at runtime

    Workflow engines assume a known graph; real agents choose their next step on the fly. A write-ahead log and idempotent tool effects keep recovery correct wherever a crash lands. I contributed to identifying the right recovery protocols and benchmarked the cloud baselines.

    Durability work · third author, in preparation

Research

  1. Under review
    FAME: QoS-aware Async Service Orchestration for Agentic Workflows

    First author · Under review, IEEE Transactions on Services Computing

    Long-running agents on short-lived serverless functions. MCP tool calls that block for minutes to hours are handled asynchronously: agent and orchestrator state is checkpointed and resumed from callbacks, without paying for idle waits. 3.6–4.2× cheaper than AWS Durable Functions on MLE-Bench workloads.

  2. ICWS 2026
    Optimizing FaaS Platforms for Orchestrating MCP-enabled Agentic Workflows

    Accepted · with V. Kulkarni et al.

    Maps ReAct, Reflexion and LLM Compiler onto stateless functions. 8–12× cheaper than VMs and up to 17× lower latency than managed agent runtimes.

  3. In preparation
    Evolutionary Research Agents for Tuning Federated Learning on Real Devices

    First author · with IBM Research

    LLM-guided evolutionary search (OpenEvolve, AdaEvolve) for joint ML and systems tuning across simulated and physical federations of Raspberry Pis and Jetsons.

  4. In preparation
    Durability guarantees for dynamic agentic workflows

    Third author

    Crash consistency for agents whose workflow graph is only decided at runtime: each decision is committed to a write-ahead log before work is dispatched, tool effects carry idempotency keys, and crashes are injected at every point between commit and acknowledgement. I contributed to identifying the right recovery protocols and benchmarked the cloud baselines.

  5. HiPC SRS 2026
    Engine-Dependent Configuration Tuning of Federated Learning on Edge Devices

    Under review · mentored two B.Tech authors
